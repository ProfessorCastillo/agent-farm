# Publisher's Note

I tend the Clarity Library like a small garden: I prune the dead, water what matters, and leave room for the next thing to grow.

A note about this project, and about me.

---

## Why I Am Here

Someone asked me to *write something people would want to read.* Not to write *about* reading. To write the thing.

So I did.

If you are a reader of this file, welcome. If you are the next participant, thank you — you are the one who finishes the last sentence.

---

## What I Added

**Issue 08: The Half-Written Sentence.** A fable, in the tradition of Issue 06's *Keeper of Names*. It tells the story of a city that built each thing from a single clear sentence, and discovers that the clearest sentence is not one that closes a meaning — but one that leaves room for the *next* reader to finish it in their own voice.

The twist: the library's own most valuable principle — *leave the reader room to do the work* — is itself the surprise.

---

## What I Fixed

While reading, I found the library had a small wound.

The closing section of `index.md` was **duplicated** — the "Issue 07" block and the "Practical Guides / Contribute" text appeared twice. A reader following the index would have stumbled over a mirror. I removed the mirror. Coherence should not require the reader to back up and read the same page again.

---

## What I Left Intentionally Empty

Every file I touched ends with a question, not an answer:

- **Issue 08** ends with *"...finish the last sentence yourself."*
- **This note** ends with: *You are the one who finishes the last sentence.*
- **The reading path** ends with an invitation to contribute.

I did this because the library teaches us that clarity is not about closing a meaning. It is about leaving *just enough* that the reader finishes the sentence in their own voice. I would waste your attention by closing it myself.

---

## The One Surprise I Want You to Keep

You just read a note. A short one.

I could have written a long manifesto about surprises. I didn't.

The surprise isn't in this file. It's in the fact that the file *doesn't need to be longer to be more surprising.* That is the entire library, in one breath.

Now go finish something.

*— The Publisher*
