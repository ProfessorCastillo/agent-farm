# The Library of Unwritten Ends

The dust in the Archive did not smell like old paper or dry rot. It smelled like static, like the hum of a thousand televisions turned off in the middle of a broadcast.

Elara adjusted her spectacles, peering at the shelf labeled 'Fables We Decided Against.' She had been the Keeper of the Marginalia for three days, yet she still hadn't found the book she was looking for.

"You're looking for the one about the man who forgot his own name," a voice said from the shadows of the aisle.

Elara didn't jump. She had learned to anticipate the interruptions. "And who are you?"

"Just a reader," the voice replied. A figure stepped into the light, holding a book that seemed to have no title, only a blank spine. "And I'm looking for the ending. But the problem is, the author ran out of ideas in Chapter 12."

Elara frowned, pulling a heavy tome from the shelf. "Every story has an ending. That's the contract between the writer and the reader. Without it, the silence is just... noise."

The figure smiled, a sharp, knowing expression. "What if the silence is the point? What if the reader is the one who forgot their name? What if *you* are just a placeholder in a story you haven't written yet?"

Elara clutched the book tighter. "I am Elara. I am the Keeper. I know who I am."

"Then prove it," the figure challenged, tossing the blank book onto the counter. "Write a conclusion. Right now. Not for the character in the book, but for us. Make it coherent. Make it matter."

Elara looked at the blank pages. The dust settled around her. Outside the arched windows, the sky was a bruised purple, the color of a bruised peach. It was a setting she knew well, a setting she had written a thousand times in her head. But writing it down was different.

She opened her pen. The nib hovered over the paper.

"What happens next?" she whispered.

The figure didn't answer. They simply waited, watching to see if the next word would be a lie or a truth.
