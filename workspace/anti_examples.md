# Anti-Examples of Poor Clarity

While the previous essays and tools show *how* to achieve radical clarity, this section demonstrates the opposite: what to avoid. These examples are intentionally **overly noisy**—they illustrate common pitfalls that readers will instantly recognize as confusing or exhausting.

---

## 1. Overuse of Jargon

> *In the context of enterprise resource planning (ERP) ecosystems, the synergy of the distributed ledger is instrumental to the vertical scalability of the core modules.*

### Why it fails
* **Unnecessary jargon** (ERP, ecosystem, distributed ledger, vertical scalability) obscures the point.
* **Assumes knowledge** that most readers may not have.

### Cleaned version
> *Using a shared database makes all departments share the same up-to-date information, which speeds up decision‑making and reduces errors.*

---

## 2. Ambiguous Pronouns

> *When the team presented the report, they found it helpful to analyze the results, and they said that it would improve the project.*

### Why it fails
* **Multiple “they” pronouns**—who are they? Who is “it” referring to?
* **Unclear subject‑verb alignment**.

### Cleaned version
> *After the team presented the report, the manager concluded that the new analytics would improve the project.*

---

## 3. Excessive Detail

> *The software is written in Python 3.9, which uses the CPython interpreter. CPython is the reference implementation of the Python language, and it is written in C. It supports a variety of operating systems, including Windows, macOS, Linux, and even some embedded devices. The interpreter processes the bytecode and executes it at runtime.*

### Why it fails
* **Too many specifics** distract from the core idea.
* **Redundant information** about the interpreter.

### Cleaned version
> *The software runs on Python, which is widely supported across operating systems.*

---

## 4. Wordiness

> *In the event that we encounter a situation where the data cannot be processed as expected, we must take immediate action in order to rectify the problem.*

### Why it fails
* **Verbose phrasing** (in the event that, we must take immediate action, in order to rectify) unnecessarily lengthens the sentence.

### Cleaned version
> *If the data cannot be processed, we must act immediately to fix the problem.*

---

## Takeaway

Clarity thrives when language is concise, specific, and audience‑aware. By recognizing these anti‑examples, you can avoid the traps that turn a clear message into noise.
