# Issue 09: The Road Not in the Catalogue

## A Tale from the Village of Every Ending

### I

There was a village called Senna where every story had already been told.

Not in the way a tired village forgets its stories. In the way a careful village *keeps* them. Every tale was written down in a great ledger: the shepherd who found a star, the baker whose bread rose into a hill, the fisherman who caught a question instead of a fish. Each one was numbered, dated, and filed in a long wooden case in the village square.

The people of Senna were proud of this. "We have told every clear story," they would say to visitors. "There is nothing left to say that has not already been said clearly."

And they were right. The ledger was complete. Every story in it was clear, well-told, and finished. No one could find a gap. No one could find a flaw.

No one could find a surprise.

### II

A child named Oren grew up in Senna. Oren was not unusual. Oren read the ledger from cover to cover by the age of five, and by eight had memorized every numbered tale.

One autumn morning, Oren stood in the square with the ledger open on a bench, and asked the eldest storyteller a question that no one had thought to ask:

"You have told every clear story. But where are the surprises?"

The storyteller smiled the way one smiles at a child who has just said something almost clever. "A surprise, little Oren, is just a story you did not expect. And we have already told every story. So there are no surprises left. We have them all. They are in the ledger."

"But I have read them all," Oren said. "And none of them surprised me."

The storyteller's smile did not change. "Then you are reading them correctly. A surprise is not in the story. A surprise is in the reader. And you, Oren, are a very good reader."

Oren closed the ledger. Oren walked to the edge of the village. Oren looked over the wall.

### III

Beyond the wall was a road.

Not a road in the ledger. Not a road in any of the numbered tales. A road that went down into a valley Oren had never seen, past a river Oren had never crossed, toward a mountain that was not in any story at all.

Oren stood there for a long time.

Then Oren went back to the storyteller.

"There is a road," Oren said. "It is not in the ledger."

The storyteller looked at the ledger. The storyteller looked at the wall. The storyteller looked at the road.

The road was there. It had always been there. No one had looked.

### IV

The people of Senna argued about what to do. Some said the road should be added to the ledger — given a number, a date, a clear ending. Some said it should be walled off, because a road that is not in the ledger is a story that has not been told clearly, and that is noise.

The storyteller said nothing for a long time.

Then the storyteller picked up the ledger, walked to the wall, and set it on the ground.

"The ledger is a list," the storyteller said. "A list is a thing that is finished. A road is a thing that is not finished. We have been so proud of our finished things that we forgot to look at the ones that are not."

Oren asked: "So the surprise was the road?"

"No," said the storyteller. "The surprise was that we had never looked."

### V

They did not add the road to the ledger. They walked it instead.

And here is the thing about roads that are not in the ledger: they do not have clear endings. They do not have numbers or dates. They have weather, and forks, and the sound of your own feet. They have the possibility that you will turn a corner and find something you did not expect, and that this thing will be real, and that it was always there.

That is what a surprise is. Not a trick. Not a flaw in the story. It is the part of the world that was waiting for someone to look without a list.

The people of Senna still have the ledger. They are still proud of it. But they no longer say "there is nothing left to say." They say: "There is a road we have not walked yet. Would you like to come?"

And sometimes, when they are very lucky, the road surprises them.
